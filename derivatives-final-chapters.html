Derivatives final: five chapters.

Your final covers Hull Chapters 11, 13, 14, 15 and 19. This page gives you each chapter in plain words, the formulas you must know by heart, the question types to expect, and two full practice papers pitched harder than the October 17 midterm.

Chapter numbers follow your course's Hull edition. In the Hull 8th edition PDF in your course pack the same chapters are 10, 12, 13, 14 and 18. These papers are original practice, not the real exam.

How hard was the midterm, and what to expect

The midterm had 5 questions × 8 points = 40 points in 2 hours, with no clarifications allowed. No question was a one-step plug-in. Each one had a twist:

Midterm questionThe twistSame twist on the final looks like
Q1 forward with dividends12 dividends that grow 1% each timeBounds or BSM with several dividends; Black's approximation
Q2 index futures lossSolve backwards for the unknown price SSolve for σ, a strike, a starting cash level, or a hedge size
Q3 margin accountFind the largest X that avoids a margin call"When is early exercise optimal?" and "is there an arbitrage?"
Q4 bond forwardCoupons inside the contract, semiannual ratesTrees with u, d from σ, options on futures or currencies
Q5 swap rateSix rates, one equation to build yourselfTwo Greeks equations solved together (gamma + vega neutral)

Expect the final to be harder: more parts per question, mixed chapters (for example a tree answer checked against Black-Scholes, or a Greek computed from BSM), and at least one "explain why" line. Write your assumptions down, since nobody will clarify during the exam.

The five chapters

Where the midterm questions came from

Your midterm was not invented from scratch. Q4 (the $920 bond with a $940 forward) is a known textbook exercise that appears online word for word. It looks like it comes from Luenberger, Investment Science (the forwards, futures and swaps chapter), and the Q5 swap with spot rates 7.0% to 8.8% has the same textbook-exercise shape. Q2 (the 200 × F index futures) circulates in online homework banks. So your professor reuses textbook exercises and past problem sets, sometimes with small changes. Expect the final to do the same with these two books:

  • Hull end-of-chapter problems for chapters 11, 13, 14, 15 and 19 (the list below).
  • Luenberger's options chapters: Models of asset dynamics (Wiener, Itô), Basic options theory (bounds, parity, lattices, early exercise) and Additional options topics (Black–Scholes, delta hedging). Luenberger is not in your course pack; borrow it from the library if you can.

I matched the midterm against web search results and the Hull PDF in your pack. Q1 and Q3 were not found word for word anywhere.
