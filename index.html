    Before the finance

    Six small ideas you can return to whenever the math feels unfamiliar.

    Percentages are parts of 100

    5% means 5 out of 100, or 0.05. To find 5% of $200, calculate 0.05 × 200 = $10. If an input says “rate (%)”, enter 5. If a formula needs a decimal rate, use 0.05.

    Growth multiplies; discounting divides

    At 10% for one year, $100 grows to 100 × 1.10 = $110. To find what that $110 is worth one year earlier at the same rate, calculate 110 ÷ 1.10 = $100.

    Powers mean repeated multiplication

    1.05² means 1.05 × 1.05. That is two years of 5% compound growth. The little number tells you how many times the growth factor is applied.

    Expected value is a weighted average

    If a fair coin pays $10 for heads and $0 for tails, its expected payment is 0.5 × 10 + 0.5 × 0 = $5. You never receive $5 in one toss; it describes the long-run average.

    Signs and units tell a story

    Positive cash flow means money received; negative means money paid, from the chosen person’s viewpoint. Always write currency and time. A yearly rate is not a monthly rate. One year is 12 months.

    A formula is a sentence with short names

    PV = FV ÷ (1 + r) says “today’s value equals next year’s money divided by its growth factor.” PV and FV are amounts, r is a decimal rate. Read the sentence before entering numbers.
