Continue beyond this midterm

The later syllabus explicitly includes options, modeling, fair-value pricing, Wiener processes, Black–Scholes derivation and Greeks. Their absence from this paper does not remove them from the course.

  1. Option strategies and parity: draw payoff and profit separately.
  2. Binomial trees: replicate a payoff, calculate pricing weights and work backward.
  3. Random processes: understand drift, volatility and time scaling. Then study the course’s Wiener/Itô derivation.
  4. Black–Scholes and Greeks: explain the hedge argument and assumptions before using the formula.

Depth still needed: the short lessons introduce these concepts; they do not fully develop Itô calculus, the BSM PDE derivation, multi-step American trees or every swap variant. Use later lectures and Hull topic readings to complete those parts. Confirm final scope when announced.

Syllabus topics outside that paper

  • Weeks 2–3: amortization, bootstrapping, fixed/floating bonds, inflation/TIPS, currencies and default. Start with loans and bonds; detailed TIPS and floating-rate conventions need the course readings.
  • Week 7: futures valuation and end-user practice. Use ETFs/futures and Derivatives practice.
  • Later term: exchange-traded options, limitations of Black–Scholes, Lintner-based option pricing, implied probability distributions, contracts, real/complex options, capital budgeting, corporate financing/capital structure and corporate risk management.

Coverage status: the existing lessons introduce smiles, real options and NPV/IRR. Full Lintner option valuation, implied-density derivation, complex options and corporate capital structure need deeper lecture/book work and future lesson expansion. Use the supplied Welch book for corporate-finance topics. Confirm whether your assessment is the final exam or individual project before planning the final revision.

Use this report-preparation sequence

  1. State the institution, exposure, decision and risk question.
  2. List input data, units, source dates and assumptions.
  3. Show typed equations and derivations; independently reproduce the numbers.
  4. Compare a baseline with stress cases, then explain limitations.
  5. Recommend controls or hedges with their costs and remaining risks.
  6. Rehearse a short explanation and questions; document sources and tools used.

This is a preparation framework derived from the syllabus’s grading priorities, not the missing individual-assignment prompt. Obtain the decks, assigned topic and rubric before drafting a submission.
